A Counsellor at the Services Division of the World Trade Organization, and my friend, gave me an idea I can't seem to get out of my head: services are embedded into goods just as goods are embedded into services. What does this mean? When you purchase a good, you do not only pay the cost of the product but also for transportation, mailing and, more recently, telecommunications. When you pay for a service, you also cover the cost of the products needed to deliver it. You need a truck, ship or plane to transport; a post box or a door to receive the mail; paper, a phone or a laptop to communicate at a distance; a router to supply Wi-Fi.

The price difference between two suppliers offering the same product is a good way to understand this concept. Why does a coffee cost $10 at Starbucks when it costs $2 at a McCafé? Or why does a fridge magnet cost more at the airport than at Ross? The gap, among other variables, lies in the service. Whether it is location, premium customer service, the delivery method or the payment facilities, these are all services.

Online selling platforms are outperforming our small and medium enterprises around the world; live online sales can generate larger revenues than thousands of retail shops. What happens now, in the era of information saturation? You can upload one photo to different e-commerce platforms and get many prices for the same product in one click. This is a change that will require us to think twice about the services we offer with our products online.

If you have not experienced buying a product from a Chinese e-commerce platform, I encourage you to try it. Dedicated customer support is available 24/7, with a human — not a bot — answering your questions. Last week I purchased a phone with the wrong storage space. Within hours of the purchase I asked for a refund, and a delivery person came to pick it up. It was the easiest refund I have ever gotten, with no cost for my mistake.

Alice, an Ecuadorian friend, worked at Temu before it even existed. She was working on marketing for Latin America on the Chinese version of the app: Pinduoduo. Now Temu is everywhere in the world, renting warehouses to receive and store goods, expanding operations and bringing other service solutions along with its e-commerce ecosystem, such as transportation and e-payments.

I am writing this from Mexico as I come back from China. I lived there from 2017 to 2023, but today, in 2026, I must admit that the e-commerce expansion in Latin America (and the world) worries me. The only solution I see to remain competitive in a time of borderless commerce is **understanding services**.
